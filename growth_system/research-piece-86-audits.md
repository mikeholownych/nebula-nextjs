# We Audited 86 Landing Pages. Not One Scored an A.
## What 86 automated conversion audits reveal about why your ads don't convert

*By Mike Holownych, Founder of Nebula Components — August 2026*

---

Over the past two months I ran 86 automated landing page audits for founders — most of them actively spending on Google, Meta, LinkedIn, or TikTok ads. Every page was scored across 9 conversion signals: message match, trust, mobile CTA, load speed, CTA clarity, above-fold content, ad signal continuity, SEO foundations, and AI readiness.

Here's what the data says.

## The headline numbers

- **86 pages audited** (founders running paid traffic, ecommerce, B2B SaaS, coaches/consultants)
- **Average score: 62.7/100**
- **Median: 61/100**
- **Best page: 77/100. Worst: 40/100.**
- **Grade distribution: 40 B's, 43 C's, 3 D's — zero A's**

Read that last line again. Not a single page out of 86 earned an A. The best page we've ever scored still leaked in at least two signals.

## The two failures on (almost) every page

Two signals failed on essentially every page we audited:

**1. Above-the-fold content — flagged on 100% of pages (86/86)**

The most common version: a headline exists, but the first viewport has no CTA, no price signal, and no reason to act. The visitor pays for your ad click, lands, and has to scroll to find out what you want them to do. Most don't scroll.

**2. Ad signal continuity — flagged on 99% of pages (85/86)**

Pages receiving paid traffic carry almost none of the signals the ad promised: no matching headline language, no campaign-consistent offer, no UTM-aware content. The ad says one thing; the page says something adjacent to it. This is the "message match" tax — and speed tools like PageSpeed Insights can't see it at all.

## The rest of the failure table

| Signal | Pages flagged | % of 86 |
|--------|--------------|---------|
| Above-fold content | 86 | 100% |
| Ad signal continuity | 85 | 99% |
| SEO foundations | 63 | 73% |
| Social proof | 41 | 48% |
| CTA clarity | 39 | 45% |
| AI readiness | 27 | 31% |
| Load speed | 25 | 29% |

Two things stand out:

**Load speed — the thing every free tool measures — was only a problem on 29% of pages.** The industry's default diagnostic (run PageSpeed, fix the score) addresses the *least common* failure. Founders are optimizing LCP while their hero section has three competing CTAs.

**Nearly half of pages (48%) have a social proof problem** — either no proof at all, or testimonials buried below the decision point.

## Quick wins outnumber major projects 2:1

Of 379 individual findings, **241 were classified as quick wins** (high impact, low effort) versus 138 major projects. The average page has ~3 fixes that take under a day each.

That's the most optimistic finding in the dataset: most conversion leaks aren't redesign problems. They're one-afternoon problems that nobody diagnosed.

## Why founders miss this

Every free audit tool in the standard stack measures a *slice*:

- PageSpeed Insights / GTmetrix → speed only (29% of the problem)
- SEOptimer → technical SEO (73%, but invisible to conversion)
- Hotjar → behavior after the leak already happened
- ChatGPT → generic advice that applies to every page on the internet ("add social proof!")

None of them answer the actual question: **does this page continue the conversation your ad started?** That's message match + above-fold + ad signals — the three signals that fail most often.

## Method notes

- All audits ran through Nebula's automated 9-signal engine (nebulacomponents.com/audit — free, no signup)
- Pages were submitted by founders or pulled from public teardown candidates
- Scores are deterministic per crawl: same page, same score
- "Flagged" = the signal produced at least one finding; findings carry effort/impact classification
- This dataset skews toward founders who suspected a problem — pages doing fine don't get audited. Selection bias runs toward worse pages, which makes the zero-A result less surprising but the 100% above-fold failure rate more damning: even self-aware founders miss it.

## The takeaway

If you're spending on ads and not converting, the odds say your problem is not your ad and not your page speed. It's that the first viewport of your page doesn't continue what the ad promised — and no speed tool will ever tell you that.
