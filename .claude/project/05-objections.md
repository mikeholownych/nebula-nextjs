# Nebula Components — Objections

## The 10 objections and the real responses

These are drawn from the actual forces at play in prospect psychology (Demand-Side Sales) and from the copy debates in the growth system files. Use these in all outreach, follow-up, and copy.

---

### 1. "I've been burned before — testing phases that go nowhere."

**Why they say it:** Every agency they've worked with has run "tests" for 3 months and delivered a deck. No implementation. No result. The $97 sounds like another way to enter that cycle.

**The real response:**
There is no testing phase. There is no call. There is no ongoing commitment. The audit runs in 60 seconds. You receive one specific finding with the evidence behind it. The Fix Pack is a one-time $97 purchase — if you don't want the fix, you never pay. If you do, it's delivered in 48 hours and you apply it yourself. You can verify the finding is real before spending a dollar.

**Copy form:** "No testing phase. No agency. No commitment. You get the specific thing that's broken, with the evidence, in 60 seconds. The $97 fix is optional — you only pay if you want the implementation."

---

### 2. "It's too expensive / I don't have budget."

**Why they say it:** Either genuinely don't have $97 (unlikely given they're spending on ads), or don't believe the ROI is real.

**The real response:**
At $97, this is not a budget decision. If you are spending $50/day on ads and converting at 0%, the question is what your current burn rate is. A founder spending $1,500/month on ads that don't convert is losing $1,403 more per month than the cost of the fix. The price isn't the barrier — the barrier is trust that the fix will work.

**Copy form:** "If you're spending more than $97/month on traffic and converting nothing, the math on the Fix Pack works in under 3 days."

---

### 3. "Not the right time."

**Why they say it:** Genuine timing issue, or avoidance. The distinction matters.

**The real response:**
The free audit takes 60 seconds and costs nothing. If you run it now, you know what's failing. You can act on it whenever the timing is right. Delaying the diagnosis doesn't stop the ad spend. Every day without the finding is another day spending on a broken page.

**Copy form:** "The audit is free and takes 60 seconds. You don't have to fix anything today — but you'll know what's wrong."

---

### 4. "I already know what's wrong with my page."

**Why they say it:** They've been told the page needs more social proof, or the headline is weak, or they've read blog posts about CRO. They have hypotheses.

**The real response:**
Most founders do have a hypothesis. The audit either confirms it — in which case you now have evidence instead of an opinion — or it finds the thing underneath the thing you were focused on. The findings are tied to raw DOM evidence, not a checklist. If you're right, the audit is a 60-second confirmation. If you're wrong, the 60 seconds saves you from fixing the wrong thing.

**Copy form:** "If you're right, the audit confirms it in 60 seconds. If there's something underneath the obvious fix, you'll find it before you build the wrong solution."

---

### 5. "I could just hire a freelancer / agency."

**Why they say it:** Familiar category. Feels more credible.

**The real response:**
A freelancer takes 2 weeks to scope and quote. An agency takes longer. Neither delivers a diagnosis in 60 seconds for $0. The audit is the step before you hire anyone — it tells you exactly what to brief them on, or whether you need them at all.

**Copy form:** "The audit is what you hand to the freelancer. It tells you exactly what to brief them on."

---

### 6. "This won't work for my industry / niche."

**Why they say it:** They believe their market is different. B2B vs B2C, or a specialist vertical.

**The real response:**
The 9 signals are structural, not industry-specific. Whether you're selling SaaS, coaching, ecommerce, or services, a headline that doesn't match your ad, a CTA that isn't visible, or no social proof near the first action are the same problem. The audit shows you what's structurally broken — not what a generic CRO consultant thinks about your niche.

**Copy form:** "The signals checked are structural. Headline match, CTA visibility, proof proximity — these fail the same way across every vertical."

---

### 7. "I've already tried optimizing the page."

**Why they say it:** They've changed the headline, tried different button colors, maybe run an A/B test. They're frustrated because nothing moved.

**The real response:**
Most page optimizations target the visible surface — headline copy, button color, layout tweaks. The audit checks the structural layer: is the CTA findable on mobile? Is there tracking on the page? Does the above-fold composition fail before the visitor reads anything? If you've tried the obvious fixes and nothing moved, the problem is usually structural, not cosmetic.

**Copy form:** "If you've already tried the obvious fixes and the number didn't move, it's usually structural. The audit checks the layer below the headline."

---

### 8. "How do I know the finding is real?"

**Why they say it:** Legitimate skepticism. Too many tools generate generic "findings."

**The real response:**
Every finding includes the measured value, the required value, the gap, and the specific DOM selector. You can verify the finding in 30 seconds with browser dev tools. The finding isn't an opinion — it's a measurable structural gap between what's on your page and what the signal requires. The engine version is stamped on every audit for traceability.

**Copy form:** "Every finding includes the DOM selector and the measured value. Ten seconds in browser dev tools confirms it."

---

### 9. "What if the fix doesn't work?"

**Why they say it:** Fear of another $97 lesson with no result.

**The real response:**
The fix addresses the specific structural finding — not a hypothesis about what might help. If the finding is real (and you can verify it before paying), the fix is the correct structural change for that finding. Whether it moves the conversion rate depends on whether the finding was the primary driver. The audit ranks findings by impact — you're fixing the highest-impact structural problem first.

**Copy form:** "You verify the finding is real before you pay. The fix is the correct structural change for that finding. The 30-day re-audit shows whether it held."

---

### 10. "I don't have a developer to apply the fix."

**Why they say it:** They think "fix" means a code change that requires a developer.

**The real response:**
Many fixes require no developer — headline rewrites, CTA copy changes, adding a meta description, moving proof above the fold. When the fix does require a code change, the kit includes the exact change written for your specific page — you can copy-paste it or hand it to a developer in 5 minutes.

**Copy form:** "Most fixes are copy or configuration changes. When code is needed, the kit includes the exact change written for your page."

---

## The silent objection (most important)

The real block at $97 is not price. It is:

> "I've been burned before. Testing phases. Agencies. Consultants. I paid and got nothing. This is probably the same."

This objection is never spoken. It activates in the first scroll. It must be addressed before the first CTA appears — not in an FAQ.

Address it by: showing the finding before asking for payment, making the audit free with no signup, and being explicit about the no-call, no-commitment structure.
