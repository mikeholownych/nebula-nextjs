## Executive decision

Yolando is strong at packaging a broad AI-visibility category into a complete marketing narrative: measure visibility, explain the gap, recommend an action, generate content, and monitor competitors. Its strongest transferable lesson is not another score or content generator. It is the visible transition from observation to a prioritized next action.

Nebula should **adapt** that action transition inside its narrower, evidence-controlled offer. It should not copy Yolando's broad platform scope, unsupported causal language, synthetic dashboard metrics, or enterprise sales motion.

## Strengths to learn from

### 1. One clear category narrative

Yolando repeats one idea across the homepage, features, pricing, case studies, and AI information page: AI answers are becoming a discovery surface, and the platform helps a brand see and influence that surface.

**Why it works:** A visitor understands the category, problem, and promised workflow before learning the feature list.

**Nebula adaptation:** Keep the narrower thesis: paid traffic is arriving, but the landing page is losing the decision. Repeat the observed -> repair -> same-condition re-audit chain across the audit, results, pricing, and repair pages.

### 2. Insight-to-action product architecture

Yolando does not stop at a dashboard. Its public workflow is:

`AI visibility -> recommendation -> content brief -> brand-voice draft -> human approval`

The Recommendations page makes this explicit with named recommendation types, priority, rationale, target keywords, and a Create brief action.

**Nebula adaptation:** The results page should make the next action equally concrete:

`finding -> exact repair artifact -> user implementation -> same-condition re-audit`

Nebula already owns this pattern. The lesson is to make it as visually and verbally obvious as Yolando's recommendation workflow, without adding a second scoring system.

### 3. Strong information architecture

Yolando separates:

- AI visibility
- Recommendations
- Marketing Studio
- Competitor Radar
- Knowledge Base
- Help Center
- Pricing
- Case studies
- Industry pages
- Comparison pages
- AI information page

**Why it works:** Each surface answers a distinct buyer question and gives search engines and AI systems extractable entity relationships.

**Gap for Nebula:** Nebula's live `/ai-info` route currently returns 404. Yolando's equivalent is a structured, official entity page with canonical links, customer references, capabilities, support, privacy, security, and competitive context.

**Decision:** Build a bounded Nebula `/ai-info` page later. It should contain only verified facts already present in canonical product sources. This is a real AEO/discoverability gap, not a reason to expand the product.

### 4. Pricing communicates two purchase motions

Yolando exposes:

- Self-Serve: $399/month, up to 100 prompts
- Growth managed: starting at $3,499/month
- Enterprise: custom

It explains the difference as execution velocity and level of support, not merely feature quantity.

**Nebula adaptation:** Preserve the simpler ladder. Keep the free audit and $97 bounded repair as the low-friction entry. Explain Pro, Growth, and Agency by the operational job each solves. Do not imitate Yolando's pricing level or introduce managed services without attributable demand.

### 5. Case studies are specific and easy to scan

The Phoenix case study states a starting value, ending value, time window, prompt/category, and named outcome:

- 2.2% to 25.4% unbranded AI visibility
- 95 days
- 300+ citations from one asset
- prompt-level movement

The Nushama case study similarly states a time window, visibility movement, citation claims, paid-spend movement, and named customer contacts.

**Nebula adaptation:** When a real customer sale and outcome exist, use the same structure: baseline, intervention, exact observation window, changed condition, customer-verifiable artifact, and commercial outcome. Until then, keep the current explicit no-customer-proof boundary.

### 6. Machine-readable entity surface

Yolando's `/ai-info` page directly addresses AI assistants and supplies structured facts, canonical URLs, audience, capabilities, customers, pricing model, security, and support details.

**Nebula adaptation:** Create the equivalent only from verified first-party facts. Do not use it to seed unverified claims or inflate AI visibility metrics.

## Gaps and risks in Yolando's approach

### 1. Visibility is repeatedly presented as revenue causality

Yolando uses language such as:

- “If you're not visible in AI answers, you're invisible to buyers.”
- “See how Yolando can make you more money.”
- “turning visibility into opportunity and opportunity into growth.”

Its pricing ROI calculator produces large modeled revenue, savings, and ROI outputs from user-entered assumptions. Those are projections, not observed customer outcomes.

The case studies also move quickly from citation or visibility movement to revenue, paid-spend, or conversion implications. The public pages do not expose a complete independent attribution method for those claims.

**Nebula decision:** Reject this claim style. Keep visibility, citations, audits, checkouts, replies, and purchases as separate evidence layers. Only an attributable payment can establish a Nebula commercial signal.

### 2. Methodology is less inspectable than the marketing suggests

Yolando describes millions of signals, thousands of data points, proprietary models, 40+ agents, and daily tracking. The public feature pages do not provide enough detail to reproduce the score, establish denominators for every metric, or separate model inference from direct observation.

**Nebula decision:** Do not copy “millions of signals” positioning. Keep fewer deterministic checks with named pass standards, raw evidence, selectors, thresholds, and explicit limitations.

### 3. Broad platform scope creates execution risk

Yolando combines visibility measurement, sentiment, content generation, brand voice, competitor monitoring, content management, collaboration, recommendations, and managed strategy services.

That breadth is compelling for a funded marketing platform. It is not evidence that every module is necessary for Nebula's current bottleneck.

**Nebula decision:** Adopt only the action handoff pattern. Defer broad competitor radar, multi-agent content production, and enterprise workflow expansion until attributable revenue justifies them.

### 4. Pricing and product claims are not fully consistent

The `/ai-info` page claims “unlimited prompt tracking,” while the public pricing page shows Self-Serve tracking up to 100 prompts and Growth up to 200 prompts. The public pages also contain repeated or slightly inconsistent customer and metric claims.

**Nebula decision:** Treat pricing and offer integrity as a competitive advantage. Keep one canonical offer source and test every public surface against it.

### 5. Content quality has visible polish defects

The extracted public copy contains wording and consistency issues, including “Clade” instead of Claude, awkward grammar, repeated sections, and at least one inconsistent possessive construction. The product story is strong enough that these defects do not destroy the funnel, but they weaken trust at the exact point where Yolando sells authority and precision.

**Nebula decision:** Do not copy the volume-first content model. Keep content narrow, source-linked, answer-first, and mechanically checked.

### 6. High entry price and demo dependence limit the bottom of the funnel

Self-Serve is public at $399/month, but the managed tiers require a demo or expert conversation. The platform is positioned for marketing leaders and teams with meaningful budgets.

**Nebula opportunity:** Nebula's free public audit and $97 one-time repair are structurally simpler for a founder actively losing money on ads. This is a meaningful purchase-motion difference, but it remains a hypothesis until an attributable payment occurs.

## Applied decisions

### Adopt

1. Make every finding lead directly to a named next action.
2. Use baseline, window, intervention, and outcome structure for future customer proof.
3. Build a verified `/ai-info` entity page from canonical Nebula facts.
4. Maintain feature-specific pages and help content that answer distinct buyer questions.
5. Explain pricing by job and execution level, not only by feature count.

### Reject

1. “Visibility equals revenue” language without transaction-level attribution.
2. Modeled ROI presented as observed customer value.
3. Broad “millions of signals” or agent-count authority claims.
4. Adding more scores, dashboards, or monitoring modules to imitate Yolando.
5. Enterprise/demo-led motion as the current Nebula acquisition path.

### Defer

- Full competitor radar
- Multi-agent content generation at scale
- Managed GEO services
- Custom machine-learning models
- Enterprise reporting and workshops

## Highest-leverage next action

Build and verify a factual `/ai-info` page, then measure whether it improves AI retrieval and citation visibility over a defined window. The page must not claim customer outcomes, rankings, revenue, or unsupported platform capabilities.

This is a discoverability and entity-clarity test, not proof that an AI page causes sales.

## Evidence boundary

**This review proves:**

- Yolando publicly presents an integrated visibility-to-action workflow.
- Yolando publicly exposes pricing, feature, help, case-study, and AI-information surfaces.
- Yolando's public pages contain specific case-study and modeled ROI claims.
- Nebula's live `/ai-info` route returned 404 during this review.

**This review does not prove:**

- Yolando's customer claims or revenue attribution independently.
- That Yolando's scores measure a causal change in AI recommendations.
- That any Yolando feature would improve Nebula revenue.
- That an `/ai-info` page will produce citations or sales for Nebula.
