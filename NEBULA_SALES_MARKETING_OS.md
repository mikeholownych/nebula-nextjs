**Derived from:** Obviously Awesome, The Mom Test, Founding Sales, Sales Pitch, Demand-Side Sales 101, Traction, The SaaS Playbook, Crossing the Chasm

## What This Is

The end-to-end, repeatable system that defines how Nebula finds, qualifies, converts, and retains customers. Every section maps a book's core principle to a Nebula executable. Nothing here is philosophy. Every layer has an artifact, a metric, and a decision rule.

This OS does not grow until revenue validates each layer. Complexity is added only when a layer breaks under real volume.

## Layer 0: The Foundation Truth

**Source:** Demand-Side Sales 101 (Moesta)

Before the system runs, anchor everything to this:

> Buyers don't wake up wanting SaaS. Something happens that makes their current situation intolerable enough to change.

**Nebula's struggling moment:**
A founder opens Stripe on a Tuesday morning. Ads are running. Clicks are arriving. The revenue number is still zero. The page "looks fine." They don't know where to look. The longer they wait, the more money disappears.

**That event — not the founder's demographic, not their industry, not their company size — is the filter.**

Every lead that has not experienced that specific event is not a Nebula lead. Every lead that has is a live buying signal.

This is why trigger-based targeting exists. This is why "doing nothing" is the primary competitor.

## Layer 1: Positioning

**Source:** Obviously Awesome (Dunford)

Positioning is the decision made before any copy is written. Get it wrong and every downstream asset compounds the error.

### The Five Positioning Components — Nebula

**1. Competitive Alternatives**
What the founder actually does instead of buying Nebula:
- Keeps running the same ads hoping performance improves
- Hires a freelancer (2-week delay, $500+ estimate, no audit rigor)
- Watches YouTube tutorials and tries to self-diagnose
- Does nothing — the most common alternative

The competitor is not a CRO agency. The competitor is indefinite delay.

**2. Differentiated Capabilities**
What Nebula does that the alternatives cannot:
- Scores a live landing page in 60 seconds across 9 conversion dimensions
- Delivers a prioritized, evidence-backed finding within 24 hours
- Ships the fix within 48 hours, not 2 weeks
- Every finding is traceable back to a specific structural problem, not an opinion

**3. Value Those Differences Create**
Not "a prioritized fix list." That is the artifact. The value is:
- The founder stops guessing
- The ad spend stops bleeding to a known-bad page
- The diagnosis is done — the specific thing that is costing them is named

**4. Who Cares Most**
Not all founders. Specifically:
- Running paid traffic (budget at risk = urgency)
- Experiencing zero or near-zero conversion (pain is active)
- Have already tried tweaking the page themselves (exhausted DIY)
- Are 1-3 months into the problem (not so new they'll wait it out, not so old they've given up)

**5. Market Context (Category)**
Not "CRO software." Not "landing page tool." The category is:
**Conversion diagnosis for founders who are already running traffic.**

The context that makes the value obvious: your ads are working. Your page is the problem. You need a diagnosis, not a platform.

### Positioning Statement (Internal)
For founders who are running paid traffic but converting nothing, who are currently stuck between "the ads are running" and "the revenue isn't coming," Nebula delivers a 60-second scored audit and a 48-hour fix — not a consultation, not a retainer, not a guess. Unlike hiring a freelancer or waiting for an agency to ramp, the finding is delivered in 24 hours and the implementation is done in 48.

## Layer 2: Sales Narrative

**Source:** Sales Pitch (Dunford)

Positioning is not copy. The sales narrative translates positioning into the sequence that makes a prospect choose Nebula over alternatives — including doing nothing.

### The Nebula Narrative Sequence

This is the structure every sales touchpoint follows. Not a script. A sequence of decisions the prospect makes in order.

**Step 1: Establish the status quo problem**
"You're running ads. Clicks are arriving. Sales aren't."

**Step 2: Name why the obvious alternatives fail**
"More ads won't fix a broken page. A freelancer takes two weeks. An agency takes more than that. Watching tutorials means guessing what to test."

**Step 3: Introduce the category**
"There's a third option: diagnose the page in 60 seconds, fix the specific things killing conversion in 48 hours."

**Step 4: Introduce Nebula**
Only after the category exists. Not "Nebula is a tool that..." — "That's what Nebula does."

**Step 5: Differentiated proof**
The audit score. The specific finding. The traceable evidence. The 48-hour implementation.

**Step 6: Customer fit signal**
"This is built for founders already spending on paid traffic who need to know exactly where the page is failing — not a generic checklist."

**Step 7: Social proof**
Evidence from real audits. Real findings. Real implementations.

### Application to Every Asset

| Asset | Entry point in sequence |
|---|---|
| Cold outreach email | Step 1 only — establish the status quo, open the gap |
| Audit delivery email | Steps 2–5 — the alternatives failed, here's the diagnosis |
| $97 offer email | Steps 5–7 — the diagnosis is done, here's the fix |
| Landing page | Steps 1–7 compressed into 400 words |
| Content post | Step 1 only — validate the problem, gate the how |

## Layer 3: ICP and Signal Intelligence

**Source:** The Mom Test (Fitzpatrick) + Demand-Side Sales 101 (Moesta)

### The Mom Test Principle Applied

The ICP is never validated by enthusiasm. It is validated by:
- Revealed behavior ("what did you do when you noticed conversions were zero?")
- Existing expenditure ("how much are you currently spending on ads?")
- Workarounds ("what have you already tried?")
- Consequences ("what happens to your business if this doesn't get fixed?")

**Every inbound reply to an audit email is a Mom Test interview opportunity.**

When a reply arrives, do not pitch immediately. Extract:
1. What triggered them to respond
2. What they've already tried
3. What they were spending when the problem started
4. What a fix would mean to them in concrete terms

This language becomes the next round of copy. No market research required — the replies are the research.

### The Struggling Moment — Segmentation Rules

**In:** Founders who have specific, named evidence of the struggling moment
- "I've been running Facebook Ads for 6 weeks and no sales"
- "We get traffic, no one converts"
- "My ROAS went from 3x to 0.8x and I don't know why"
- "I'm spending $50/day and getting clicks, maybe 2-3 sales a week"

**Out:** Founders who might have the problem someday
- "We're planning to launch ads next month"
- "We want to optimize our site"
- "We're looking at conversion tools"

**Qualification gate:** Has this person spent real money on traffic and experienced zero or near-zero conversion? If yes, they qualify. Everything else is education, not sales.

### Forces of Change (Demand-Side Sales)

Four forces determine whether a prospect buys:

**Push forces (away from status quo — amplify these):**
- Money is leaving and not coming back (ad spend)
- The problem has been active for weeks or months
- DIY attempts haven't worked

**Pull forces (toward Nebula — make these concrete):**
- The audit is immediate, not a 2-week engagement
- The price is $97, not $5,000
- The finding is specific, not a generic checklist

**Anxiety forces (toward inaction — address these):**
- "Will this actually work for my industry?"
- "What if the fix doesn't work?"
- "Is this just another tool I'll pay for and not use?"

**Habit forces (toward doing nothing — name these):**
- "I've been hoping the ads will improve"
- "I've tried fixing the headline already"
- "I'll look at this when things are slower"

Every audit email addresses all four forces. The finding amplifies push. The offer amplifies pull. The 48-hour guarantee addresses anxiety. Naming the cost of delay addresses habit.

## Layer 4: Channel Architecture

**Source:** Traction (Weinberg + Mares) + The SaaS Playbook (Walling)

### The Bullseye Framework — Nebula 2026

The principle: run cheap experiments across several channels before committing six months to one. A channel that works is identified by payment, not by traffic or reply rates.

**Current beachhead — Inner ring (active bets):**

| Channel | Status | Current cost | Signal metric |
|---|---|---|---|
| Trigger-aware outbound email | Active | ~$0/send | Replies → audit requests → payments |
| Public teardowns (content) | Active | Time only | Audit starts from organic |
| Founder community posts (ICP-present forums) | Active | Time only | Inbound audit requests |

**Middle ring (worth testing next):**

| Channel | Test cost | Trigger |
|---|---|---|
| LinkedIn DM (post-engager) | Time | When first 3 outbound payments confirmed |
| Cold email at scale (Instantly.ai) | $97/mo | When manual outbound conversion rate is known |
| Twitter/X posts | Time | Parallel to current content |

**Outer ring (do not touch yet):**
- Paid ads (ironic — don't run ads until conversion is proven)
- SEO/content at scale (compounding channel, 6-month lag)
- Partnerships / integrations

**Decision rule:** A channel moves from middle to inner only after producing at least one attributable payment per 50 contacts reached. A channel stays in the outer ring until all inner ring channels are saturated.

### The SaaS Playbook — Funnel Discipline

Each funnel stage has one and only one metric that determines whether to optimize or move on:

| Stage | Metric | Target | Action if below |
|---|---|---|---|
| Trigger detection | Qualified signals per week | 10+ | Expand source queries |
| Audit delivery | Delivery rate | >80% of qualified | Fix MailCheck/Hunter gate |
| Email open | Open rate | >40% (cold trigger email) | Test subject lines |
| Audit engagement | Reply or click | >15% | Rewrite audit email copy |
| Offer conversion | $97 payment | >3% of audit-engaged | Rewrite offer email |
| Re-audit / upsell | Return within 60 days | Track | Retainer offer trigger |

**Walling's funnel discipline:** Fix the top of the funnel before optimizing the bottom. Zero payments means the problem is either (1) not enough qualified leads reaching the audit, or (2) the audit email isn't converting to reply/click. Diagnose the correct stage before acting.

## Layer 5: Outreach and Sales Motion

**Source:** Founding Sales (Kazanjy)

### The Non-Negotiable Principle

The founder must personally establish a working sales motion before any automation is layered on top. Automation before a working motion amplifies failure, not success.

A working motion means:
- A specific type of person (trigger-qualified founder)
- Reached through a specific channel (outbound email from trigger signal)
- With a specific message (audit finding, evidence-based)
- Converts at a measurable rate (at least 1 in 50 reaches payment)

Until that rate exists, every new infrastructure investment is a distraction.

### The Nebula Sales Motion — Step by Step

**Stage 1: Signal Detection**
- Source: public signals (HN Show, IH posts, Product Hunt launches, LinkedIn posts)
- Qualification: is there a struggling moment present?
- Output: domain + company name + founding trigger

**Stage 2: Live Audit**
- Run `deliver_audit.py` against the domain
- Score across 9 conversion dimensions
- Extract the single most damaging finding
- Output: audit score + primary finding + secondary finding

**Stage 3: Risk Check**
- Hunter + MailCheck contactability verification
- Block: CONFIRMED_INVALID, ACCEPT_ALL (unless override), undeliverable
- Output: cleared contact + deliverability confidence

**Stage 4: First Touch Email**
- Under 100 words
- Subject: company-specific, under 6 words
- Open: trigger context (why this person, why now)
- Bridge: one specific audit finding
- CTA: "Want me to run one on [domain]?" — not a checkout link
- Governed send: Mon–Fri 08:00–17:00 ET only

**Stage 5: Follow-Up Sequence**
- Day 3 (FU1): Different angle, not a resend. Secondary finding or industry comparison.
- Day 7 (FU2): Value-add. What this finding costs in real numbers.
- Day 14 (FU3): Permission close. "Closing the loop — worth a look or not?"
- Day 30 (Recircle): New finding if audit has changed. Different subject, different angle.

**Stage 6: Reply Classification**
Every reply falls into one category. Route accordingly.

| Reply type | Action |
|---|---|
| Interested / wants audit | Send full audit immediately, offer $97 within 24h |
| Question about the finding | Answer specifically, then offer |
| "Send it" / "sounds good" | Deliver audit, $97 offer in same email |
| "Not right now" | Log, recircle in 30 days |
| "Not relevant" | Suppress permanently |
| No reply at all | Proceed through sequence |

**Stage 7: $97 Offer**
After audit engagement. Not before.
- Email opens with the finding (recognition beat)
- Names the cost of inaction (one sentence)
- Offers the fix: $97, done in 48 hours, specific implementation
- Single Stripe checkout link
- No upsell until payment is confirmed

**Stage 8: Delivery and Follow-Up**
- Manual implementation delivery within 48 hours of payment
- Re-audit the page 30 days post-implementation
- Share the before/after publicly (with permission) — this is the content engine
- Flag for retainer offer if ROAS has improved

### Pipeline Stages (CRM Discipline)

| Stage | Definition | Exit criteria |
|---|---|---|
| Signal | Trigger detected, no contact yet | Audit completed |
| Audit Sent | Audit email delivered | Reply or 14 days |
| Engaged | Reply received | $97 offer sent |
| Offer Sent | $97 offer delivered | Payment or 7 days |
| Closed Won | Payment confirmed in Stripe | Implementation delivered |
| Closed Lost | No reply after full sequence | Recircle after 30 days |

**Kazanjy's discipline:** Update stage the day it changes. A pipeline that isn't updated is a fiction.

### The Three Copy Failures to Prevent

**1. Describing the artifact instead of the emotional payoff**
Wrong: "You receive a prioritized fix list across 9 conversion dimensions."
Right: "You stop guessing. The specific thing killing your conversion has a name."

**2. Naming the solution before the problem is felt**
Wrong: Opening with what Nebula does.

**3. Closing the information loop before the click**
Wrong: "Here are the three fixes: [list]"

## Layer 7: Early Market Discipline

**Source:** Crossing the Chasm (Moore)

### Where Nebula Is Now

Early adopters. Not mainstream. The distinction matters for every decision.

**Early adopters** (current customers):
- Willing to tolerate incompleteness
- Motivated by the possibility of solving the problem, not proven references
- Will overlook rough edges if the core finding is right
- Do not need a case study — they need a credible diagnosis

**Mainstream customers** (not yet in scope):
- Need proof: customer names, before/after numbers, industry-specific references
- Need a "whole product" — implementation, support, repeatability, docs
- Will not tolerate incompleteness

**Current mandate:** Build the beachhead. Get 5 paying customers in the exact same niche (SaaS founders running paid ads). Do not dilute to ecommerce, agencies, or B2B service companies yet.

### The Chasm Warning

Kazanjy and Moore align here: a handful of enthusiastic early adopters is not evidence that the mainstream is ready. Before reading positive early signals as "product-market fit," confirm:
- Payment is confirmed in Stripe (not enthusiasm, not "I'll definitely try this")
- The customer fits the ICP exactly (not adjacent)
- The implementation actually worked (re-audit confirmed improvement)

The test for "crossing to mainstream" is: do we have 5 customers we can reference by name, with specific before/after numbers, in the same niche? Until yes, we are still in early adopter mode.

## Layer 8: The Weekly Decision Loop

**Source:** The SaaS Playbook (Walling) + Founding Sales (Kazanjy)

### Every Week — Non-Negotiable

**Monday: Pipeline state**
Review the CRM. How many in each stage? What moved? What stalled?

Single question: what is the bottleneck?

| Bottleneck | Action |
|---|---|
| Zero new signals | Expand source queries or add a new trigger type |
| Signals but no audits | Check audit delivery pipeline |
| Audits sent but no replies | Rewrite the audit email |
| Replies but no payments | Rewrite the $97 offer |
| Payments but no repeat | Add retainer offer |

**Wednesday: Evidence audit**
Pull the last 7 days of inbound replies. Extract language. Look for:
- Phrases the ICP uses that don't appear in our copy (update copy)
- New struggling moments described (update signal detection queries)
- Objections raised (address in follow-up sequence)

**Friday: Channel experiment**
Run one test per week. One variable. One metric. Never change two things at once.

Examples:
- Different subject line format on 20 sends vs current subject on 20 sends
- Different trigger source (IH vs Product Hunt) — which produces higher reply rate?
- Different opening line (trigger-first vs finding-first) — which gets more replies?

Record the result. A channel experiment produces one binary decision: run more of this or stop.

### Metrics Hierarchy

**Revenue metrics (these are the scoreboard):**
- Payments this week: $
- Total revenue: $
- Payments to date: N

**Commercial metrics (these diagnose the scoreboard):**
- Qualified signals detected
- Audits delivered
- Replies received
- $97 offers sent
- Checkout starts
- Payments

**Operational metrics (these protect the scoreboard):**
- MailCheck hard blocks
- Bounced emails
- Opt-outs
- Sender health

**Never aggregate these three columns.** Revenue is the only metric that validates the system. Commercial and operational metrics explain why the revenue number is what it is.

## Layer 9: Retaining and Crossing Early Customers

**Source:** Founding Sales (Kazanjy) + The SaaS Playbook (Walling)

### After First Payment

The sale is not the end of the motion. It is the beginning of the evidence base.

1. **Deliver within 48 hours.** No exceptions. This is the product promise.
2. **Document everything.** Before audit score. After implementation. Specific changes made.
3. **Re-audit at Day 30.** Compare scores. Share the delta.
4. **Ask for three things:**
   - A quote (for social proof)
   - Permission to publish the before/after (for content)
   - An introduction to one other founder with the same problem (for referrals)

5. **Offer the retainer.** "Your page scored X before, Y after. If you're scaling spend again, this will drift. $197/month keeps it dialed." The retainer offer is earned by the first implementation working. Do not offer it without evidence.

### The Case Study as Growth Infrastructure

Every completed implementation is a case study. Case studies are the mechanism for crossing the chasm. Without them, the mainstream market will not move.

**Minimum viable case study:**
- Before audit score + primary finding
- Specific implementation (what changed)
- After audit score
- Before/after conversion metric (if available)
- One sentence from the customer

Ten of these, published publicly, becomes the proof base that makes cold outreach response rates meaningfully higher.

## What This OS Does Not Do

- It does not automate outreach before a working manual motion is confirmed (Kazanjy)
- It does not spend on paid acquisition before organic conversion is proven (Walling)
- It does not scale to mainstream before the early adopter beachhead is established (Moore)
- It does not add channels before existing channels produce at least one attributable payment per 50 contacts (Traction)
- It does not interpret enthusiasm, replies, or audit requests as revenue (Mom Test)
- It does not pitch before the ICP's struggling moment is named and validated (Demand-Side Sales)
- It does not present the solution before the problem is felt (Sales Pitch)
- It does not define the ICP by demographics instead of behavior (Obviously Awesome)
