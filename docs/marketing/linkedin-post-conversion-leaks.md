## POST

I audited a landing page last week that was spending $2,400/month on Google Ads.

The page looked good. Clean design, professional copy, clear CTA.

But six seconds after landing, I still had no idea what the product actually did for me.

The headline wasn't obviously bad either:

"Smarter workflow automation for scaling teams."

The problem was that it assumed I already understood the problem the product was solving.

That might work for someone who already knows the category, has done their research, and is comparing vendors.

That's not necessarily the person clicking an ad for the first time.

Cold traffic usually arrives with very little context and even less patience. Before someone cares what the product does, they need to recognize that the page is talking about their problem.

That's the distinction I see missed over and over.

Most pages answer:

"What does this product do?"

When the visitor is still trying to answer:

"Is this actually for me?"

In this case, the ad may have done its job perfectly well. It got the right person to the page.

The page just continued the conversation from three steps too far ahead.

I put together a short guide on the 7 places this shows up.

If this was useful, like it, share it with someone running paid traffic, and save it for the next time you're reviewing a landing page.
