# TikTok Script Format

## The 30-Second Structure

| Timestamp | Section | Purpose |
|-----------|---------|---------|
| 0–3s | **HOOK** | Pattern interrupt. One fact. No intro. |
| 3–10s | **PROBLEM** | Why this is costing them money. Visceral. |
| 10–25s | **REVEAL** | What's actually happening + the fix. |
| 25–30s | **CTA** | Low friction. No hard sell. |

---

## Worked Example (CTA finding)

```
HOOK (0–3s):
[Screen: Chrome DevTools open on a live landing page]
"This button is costing you 40% of your revenue."

PROBLEM (3–10s):
[Zoom to button: "Get Started"]
You're paying $3 per click. They land here. They see 'Get Started.'
Their brain skips it. They don't know what happens when they click.
They leave.

REVEAL (10–25s):
[Screen: Inspect element, change button text in real time to "Run my free audit"]
Change three words. That's it.
Action verb plus what they get. The user now knows the transaction.
Every test we've run: specific CTA outperforms generic by 50 to 70 percent.

CTA (25–30s):
Go check your button right now. View page source, search for your CTA text.
If it's "Get Started" - you already know what to do.
```

---

## Screen Recording Checklist

The format requires visual proof. Each script should specify what to show:

- **DevTools findings:** Open Elements tab, highlight the broken element
- **Source view:** Ctrl+U → Ctrl+F → show the missing/broken tag
- **Live demo:** Change a value in DevTools, show the before/after
- **Score reveal:** Show audit results page with the specific finding highlighted

---

## Voice Checklist

- [ ] Hook is under 10 words
- [ ] Demonstrates something visual on screen
- [ ] Problem connects to money leaving their account
- [ ] Reveal is a specific fix, not general advice
- [ ] CTA is one action, not a product pitch
